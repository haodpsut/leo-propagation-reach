\begin{abstract}
A preliminary version of this study reported, from \numPrelimSeeds{} seeds, that the
transition-probability kernel of a continuous-time quantum walk, $|e^{-it\Lap}|^{2}$, outperforms
the heat kernel $e^{-t\Lap}$ as a propagation operator on a 1584-satellite low-Earth-orbit
inter-satellite-link graph. This paper audits that claim. Three operators sharing one eigenbasis and one network are compared on Walker-delta
shells of 264 to 4400 satellites, two node-field tasks and ten seeds, across arms that vary the
parameterisation and initialisation of the learned propagation time $t$, the capacity, the
budget and the link pattern, against fixed-reach baselines whose reach is measured. Through a misread generator flag that both
versions share, the main grids are on the full torus, which no constellation realises; the graph both
versions described is a separate arm. Under the preliminary budget no operator
improves on a constant predictor by more than \numGainLargeMax\% on any shell of 1584 satellites
or more. With a five-fold budget, on the torus, the heat kernel is better on the smallest shell on
both tasks, two dependent tests at the smallest corrected $p$ the design can return, while at
1584 satellites the paired tests resolve a difference \numMdeRatioHopsShellOne{} times the one
under audit, so the torus is silent on it. On the seam-cut graph the preliminary direction at
1584 satellites is recovered at the corrected level, on nine seeds, on one of two metrics; a
control that removes as many links at random positions moves the cell in the same direction but
below its own resolution, so the seam is not necessary for the direction and the mechanism is
not identified. A
learned $t$ beats the best tuned fixed-reach baseline by \numLadderGapBestPctHopsShellOne\% in its
best arm and loses in its worst, and which long runs collapse changes with the floating-point
precision. All code, results and the dated protocol are released.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The inter-satellite-link (ISL) graph of a low-Earth-orbit (LEO) mega-constellation is large and
nearly regular, and its links across the seam and the polar regions come and
go~\cite{plusgrid,starlink}. Learned models that propagate information over this graph have been
proposed for routing~\cite{lira,deepladu} and for connectivity decisions~\cite{comsat}. At each
layer such a model applies a propagation operator $\Phiop(\Lap)$ to node features and then a
shared nonlinearity~\cite{gcn,shuman}, so the choice of operator is a design decision whose
consequences grow with the graph.

Three operators span the design space studied here. The local step of a graph convolutional
network~\cite{gcn}, equation~\eqref{eq:gcn} below, moves information one hop per layer. The heat
kernel $e^{-t\Lap}$ moves it diffusively, over a range that grows like $\sqrt{t}$~\cite{grand}.
The transition-probability kernel of a continuous-time quantum walk~\cite{farhi},
$|e^{-it\Lap}|^{2}$, is a real, row-stochastic matrix whose rows spread ballistically, over a range
that grows like $t$, and it can be dropped into the same layer in place of the heat
kernel~\cite{qwnn,qgnn}. Recent work has made a separate and stronger argument for walk-based
operators: the unitary propagator $e^{-it\Lap}$ applied to features damps no frequency and
preserves feature norm, so it resists over-smoothing~\cite{ctqwgnn,ctqwformer,graphqwalk}. That
argument concerns the unitary operator in feature space. It is not tested here, and we say so at
the outset: the probability kernel studied in this paper is, like the heat kernel, a real
nonnegative row-stochastic matrix, and it inherits from the walk only the shape of its rows
(Section~\ref{sec:operators}). What the probability kernel might still offer is longer reach at
equal $t$, and whether that translates into a better propagation operator once $t$ is learned is
an empirical question.

A preliminary version of this study, circulated for review in June 2026 and available in the
companion repository, answered that question in the affirmative: on a 1584-satellite shell the
walk kernel reached a mean absolute error of \numPrelimWalkMAE{} against \numPrelimHeatMAE{} for the
heat kernel, from \numPrelimSeeds{} seeds, and the trend from smaller shells was read as the
ballistic mechanism emerging with scale. This paper is a reproducibility audit of that result. It
asks whether the ranking is a property of the two kernels or of the way they were evaluated, and
it answers by making explicit and varying the quantities the preliminary comparison left
implicit: the training budget relative to graph size, the parameterisation and initialisation of
the learned propagation time $t$, the capacity, the number of seeds, the inferential rule by
which a winner is declared, and, as it turned out, the graph itself. One fact about the graph
governs how the rest of the paper must be read. The preliminary version and the first drafts of
this audit both described a +Grid with its counter-rotating seam cut; the generator's flag was
misread in both, and every run of the two main grids, \numTotalRuns{} in all, is on the full
4-regular torus, a graph that no constellation realises (Sections~\ref{sec:graphs}
and~\ref{sec:threats}). The main grids are reported as what they are, a study of the operators on
the torus. The graph both versions intended is run in a separate arm at the larger budget
(Section~\ref{sec:seam}), and every claim of this paper that is specific to LEO
inter-satellite-link graphs rests on that arm alone.

The contribution is the audit and what it finds, and what it adds to the correction of one
unpublished report is a method for reading such comparisons that is itself measured: the false-positive
rate of the vote rule the preliminary protocol used, the minimum effect the paired tests can
detect, the reach of every operator on the graph, and the sensitivity of a single run to the
arithmetic.
\begin{itemize}
\item A protocol in which the operators share one eigendecomposition, one network, one parameter
  count and one training loop; in which budget, $t$ parameterisation, $t$ initialisation,
  capacity and the link pattern of the graph are arms; in which fixed-reach decoupled propagation
  baselines, personalised PageRank and powers of the normalised adjacency, are run over a ladder
  whose reach is measured on the graph and bracket the learned reach of the kernels; and in
  which every reading rule is stated with its operating characteristics, including the
  minimum detectable effect of the corrected paired test, before a verdict is read
  (Section~\ref{sec:protocol}).
\item Under the preliminary budget of 200 epochs, no operator improves on the constant predictor by
  more than \numGainLargeMax\% on any shell of 1584 satellites or more, in any arm, at either
  capacity (Section~\ref{sec:floor}); the sensitivity of this statement to the floor threshold is
  tabulated. On the one shell where learning occurs the heat-versus-walk ranking changes with the
  $t$ arm, through a seed-level bimodality in which the two kernels escape the constant solution
  at indistinguishable rates (Section~\ref{sec:hidden}).
\item With a budget of 1000 epochs every shell leaves the floor. On the torus the heat kernel is
  better on the 264-satellite shell on both tasks, two dependent tests on one shell that both
  return the smallest corrected $p$ the design can produce (\numPooledMinHolmSix), one of them
  on a task that on the torus has no held-out content. On the larger shells the paired tests
  certify no direction, and at 1584 satellites on the hop field their minimum detectable effect,
  \numPooledHopsShellOneMde, is \numMdeRatioHopsShellOne{} times the difference of
  \numPrelimDiff{} the preliminary version reported: the torus grid cannot adjudicate the claim
  it set out to audit at that claim's own scale (Section~\ref{sec:budget}). A per-cell test over
  \numGthreeArmCells{} arm-level cells, the first draft's headline, cannot reject in
  \numPowerCannotReject{} of them at any effect size and is reported as description. Against the
  best tuned fixed-reach baseline, a learned propagation time in its best arm is ahead by
  \numLadderGapBestPctHopsShellOne\% at 1584 satellites on the hop field and behind in its worst
  arm; the first draft's 53\% margin was measured against a baseline that had half-failed to
  train.
\item On the seam-cut graph the preliminary direction at 1584 satellites on the hop field is
  recovered at the corrected level, on nine seeds, on the MAE metric only; on the far-node metric
  the preliminary version used, and which should favour reach, nothing is certified on that
  graph. A control that removes as many links at random positions moves the cell in the same
  direction, below its own corrected resolution and uncorrected, with one of four arms reversed:
  the seam is not necessary for the direction, and the held-out-content mechanism proposed for
  it is contradicted by the control's own training-to-test separation. The other five
  (task, shell) pairs read as on the torus (Section~\ref{sec:seam}).
\item Long training collapses \numGridThreePathologyPct\% of runs to the constant solution,
  counted rather than dropped, and which runs collapse depends on the floating-point precision:
  the same seed escapes in one precision and collapses in the other; the three certified
  verdicts rerun in double precision all hold (Section~\ref{sec:collapse}).
\item The released artifact of the preliminary version used a network of \numPrelimParams{}
  parameters while its released code defaults to \numReleasedParams{}, with no script for the
  experiment in question; and both versions misread the generator's seam flag, so that every
  main-grid run of this audit is on the full torus (Section~\ref{sec:threats}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Propagation operators and their reach}
Spectral graph convolutions began as polynomial filters of the Laplacian~\cite{chebnet,shuman};
the graph convolutional network of Kipf and Welling~\cite{gcn} keeps the first-order term and
applies a normalised adjacency once per layer, so depth bounds reach. Stacking layers drives
features toward a constant, the over-smoothing effect identified by Li et al.~\cite{lihanwu},
shown by Oono and Suzuki~\cite{oono} to be exponential in depth, and surveyed by Rusch et
al.~\cite{oversmooth}; remedies include decoupling propagation from transformation as in
SGC~\cite{sgc} and personalised-PageRank propagation~\cite{appnp}, and residual and identity
terms as in GCNII~\cite{gcnii}. Alon and Yahav~\cite{oversquash} describe the complementary
bottleneck by which information from exponentially many distant nodes is squashed into
fixed-size vectors, which Topping et al.~\cite{topping} relate to negative curvature of the
graph. Continuous-time views
replace the discrete step with a differential equation on the graph: GRAND~\cite{grand} uses
diffusion, whose solution is the heat kernel; PDE-GCN~\cite{pdegcn} and graph-coupled
oscillator networks~\cite{graphcon} use wave-type dynamics, whose reach grows linearly in
time rather than as a square root. The distinction between diffusive and ballistic spreading
that motivates the walk kernel in this paper is the same distinction, and the wave-type models
are its classical form.

\paragraph{Quantum walks as graph operators}
The continuous-time quantum walk of Farhi and Gutmann~\cite{farhi} evolves an amplitude
vector by $e^{-it\Lap}$ and spreads ballistically; Kempe~\cite{kempe} and M\"ulken and
Blumen~\cite{mulken} review the walk and its transport properties on complex networks, and
Childs~\cite{childs} shows it is computationally universal. Dernbach et al.~\cite{qwnn} and Verdon et
al.~\cite{qgnn} were among the first to place walk-derived operators inside trainable graph
models. GraphQWalk~\cite{graphqwalk} builds unsupervised, position-independent structural
embeddings from walk transition probabilities. CTQWformer~\cite{ctqwformer} learns the walk
Hamiltonian and feeds final-time probabilities into a graph transformer. CTQW-GNN~\cite{ctqwgnn}, a preprint at the time of writing,
uses the unitary propagator itself in feature space and proves that, because its eigenvalues lie
on the unit circle, no frequency is damped and the Dirichlet energy of the features cannot decay
exponentially with depth. That result concerns the unitary operator acting on features. The
operator studied here is the transition-probability kernel $|e^{-it\Lap}|^{2}$, a real
nonnegative matrix that shares the ballistic profile of the walk but not the norm-preserving
property; the distinction matters for interpreting the results
(Section~\ref{sec:discussion}). Quantum walks on communication networks have also been used
directly, for instance the Hadamard-walk model for network identification of
Liang et al.~\cite{hadamard}.

\paragraph{Graph learning on LEO constellations}
Handley~\cite{handley} first argued that inter-satellite routing in a mega-constellation is
a latency question, Bhattacherjee and Singla~\cite{plusgrid} fix the +Grid inter-satellite
topology that most later work assumes, Kassing et al.~\cite{hypatia} provide the Hypatia
simulator for such networks, and del Portillo et al.~\cite{starlink} compare the announced
constellations, including the Starlink shell whose geometry the 1584-satellite graph here
reproduces. Learned models on this graph include the link-identified routing architecture of
Zhang et al.~\cite{lira}, the duality-guided graph learning for joint connectivity and routing
of Gu et al.~\cite{deepladu}, and machine-learning-driven cellular-satellite
multi-connectivity~\cite{comsat}. None of these compares propagation operators under one training protocol
with the reach of every operator, learned or fixed, measured on the graph rather than asserted,
which is the gap this study addresses. The tasks used here are
deliberately decoupled from any particular routing or traffic-engineering system, so that the
propagation question is isolated from questions of load.

\paragraph{Evaluation pitfalls}
Shchur et al.~\cite{shchur} showed that the ranking of graph neural network architectures on
standard benchmarks changes with the data split and the seed, Henderson et al.~\cite{henderson}
made the corresponding point for deep reinforcement learning, where few seeds and unreported
tuning produce rankings that do not replicate, Bouthillier et al.~\cite{bouthillier} quantify
how much benchmark variance comes from sources other than the method, and Pineau et
al.~\cite{pineau} report on the reproducibility programme that turned such findings into
checklists. The present study applies the same discipline
to a comparison of propagation operators, and adds two quantities specific to spectral
operators, the training budget relative to graph size and the parameterisation of the
learned propagation time.

\section{Setting}
\label{sec:setting}

\subsection{Constellation graphs}
\label{sec:graphs}

Each graph is a snapshot of a Walker-delta shell. Satellite positions follow circular-orbit
propagation in an Earth-centred inertial frame; inter-satellite links follow the +Grid
pattern~\cite{plusgrid}, two intra-plane and two inter-plane links per satellite. The generator
suppresses inter-plane links above 70$^{\circ}$ latitude and can remove them across the
counter-rotating seam. In every run of the two main grids the seam links are \emph{kept}, and at
53$^{\circ}$ inclination no satellite reaches 70$^{\circ}$, so every snapshot of the main grids is
the full 4-regular torus of $P\times S$ satellites and $2N$ links; snapshots differ only in their
link lengths. The preliminary version and the first drafts of this paper stated that the seam was
cut; it was not, in either, and Section~\ref{sec:threats} records how the generator's flag was
misread. A seam-cut arm, in which the inter-plane links between the last and the first plane are
removed so that $2S$ satellites drop to degree three and the graph is no longer vertex-transitive,
is run at the larger budget and reported in Section~\ref{sec:seam}. The graph is restricted to its
largest connected component. Four shells are used (Table~\ref{tab:shells}). The 264-satellite shell is the main scale of the preliminary
version; the 1584-satellite shell reproduces the geometry of the first Starlink
shell~\cite{starlink}, 72 planes of 22 satellites at 53$^{\circ}$ and 550~km; the 3168- and
4400-satellite shells are scale-ups of that geometry, 72 planes of 44 and 100 planes of 44,
and are synthetic in the sense that no operator flies them. They exist to make the diameter
grow.

Each training or evaluation instance draws a snapshot time uniformly over the orbital period
and a destination satellite uniformly over the component. Instance construction is seeded, so
every instance is reproducible from its index.

\subsection{Three operators on one eigenbasis}
\label{sec:operators}

Let $\Lap = \mathbf{D} - \mathbf{A}$ be the combinatorial Laplacian of the snapshot, with
eigendecomposition $\Lap = \mathbf{U}\,\mathrm{diag}(\lambda)\,\mathbf{U}^{\top}$ computed once
per instance and shared by all operators. The three operators are
\begin{align}
\Phiop_{\mathrm{gcn}} &= \tilde{\mathbf{D}}^{-1/2}(\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{I})\tilde{\mathbf{D}}^{-1/2}, \label{eq:gcn}\\
\Phiop_{\mathrm{heat}}(t) &= \mathbf{U}\,\mathrm{diag}\!\left(e^{-t\lambda}\right)\mathbf{U}^{\top}
  = e^{-t\Lap}, \label{eq:heat}\\
\Phiop_{\mathrm{walk}}(t) &= \left|\mathbf{U}\,\mathrm{diag}\!\left(e^{-it\lambda}\right)\mathbf{U}^{\top}\right|^{2}
  = \left|e^{-it\Lap}\right|^{2}, \label{eq:walk}
\end{align}
where the square in~\eqref{eq:walk} is taken entrywise on the modulus, so that
$[\Phiop_{\mathrm{walk}}]_{ij}$ is the probability that a continuous-time quantum walk started
at $j$ is found at $i$ after time $t$. This choice fixes what the study can and cannot say.
Both~\eqref{eq:heat} and~\eqref{eq:walk} are real, nonnegative and row-stochastic; applied to a
feature matrix, both average neighbouring features with nonnegative weights and both damp
high-frequency components. The unitary propagator $e^{-it\Lap}$ of CTQW-GNN~\cite{ctqwgnn} is
neither of these things, and its over-smoothing argument does not transfer to~\eqref{eq:walk}
and is not tested here. The two kernels differ in the profile of the row as a function of graph
distance, monotone for the heat kernel and oscillatory for the walk kernel, and in how that
profile's width grows with $t$; given that the network trains $t$, it is plausible from the
definitions alone that the two will be hard to tell apart, and the experiments below are the
test of that expectation, not of the unitary argument.

Figure~\ref{fig:spreading} makes the difference concrete on a path graph, where distance from
the source is a single coordinate. A row of the heat kernel is a bell whose spatial variance grows
as $t^{\numSpreadExpHeat}$; a row of the walk kernel is a pair of fronts whose variance grows as
$t^{\numSpreadExpWalk}$, with oscillations between them. The exponents are fitted from the
kernels themselves. This is the ballistic-versus-diffusive distinction in its cleanest form: at
equal $t$ the walk reaches farther, and its reach grows faster with $t$. Whether that translates
into a better propagation operator once $t$ is learned is the empirical question of this paper.

\subsection{Network, parameterisation of \texorpdfstring{$t$}{t}, and tasks}
\label{sec:network}

The network has $K$ layers, each computing
$\mathbf{H}^{(\ell+1)} = \sigma\!\left(\Phiop^{(\ell)}\,\mathbf{H}^{(\ell)}\mathbf{W}^{(\ell)}\right)$
with a ReLU nonlinearity and a linear read-out to one scalar per node. For the two spectral
operators, each layer carries its own scalar propagation time $t_{\ell}$, trained jointly with
the weights. The preliminary version parameterised it as $t_{\ell}=\mathrm{relu}(\theta_{\ell})$
with $\theta_{\ell}$ initialised at 0.5. Section~\ref{sec:protocol} explains why this is an arm
of the present study and adds $t_{\ell}=\mathrm{softplus}(\theta_{\ell})$, which keeps
$t_{\ell}>0$ with a live gradient, initialised so that the two parameterisations start at the
same value. The GCN operator has no $t$.

The input to every node is two features, an indicator of the destination and the normalised
degree. Two node-field targets are used. With $d$ the destination, $h(i,d)$ the geodesic hop count
and $\tau(i,d)$ the shortest-path propagation delay computed on the inter-satellite link
lengths, the targets are
\begin{equation}
y^{\mathrm{hop}}_{i} = \frac{h(i,d)}{\max_{j} h(j,d)}, \qquad
y^{\mathrm{delay}}_{i} = \frac{\tau(i,d)}{\max_{j} \tau(j,d)},
\label{eq:tasks}
\end{equation}
so that both lie in $[0,1]$ and the constant predictor has the same meaning on every shell. The
hop field is the task of the preliminary version. Both fields in~\eqref{eq:tasks}
require the model to move a single seeded signal across the graph, which is the propagation
question in isolation; neither involves traffic or load. The metric is the mean absolute
error (MAE) over nodes on the evaluation instances of a seed, which are drawn from the same
generator as its training instances with a different destination and snapshot time.

One property of the torus must be stated here because it decides what the evaluation instances
measure. A torus is vertex-transitive, so the hop field from any destination is the same field up
to a relabelling of the nodes, and the network, whose only inputs are the destination indicator
and the (constant) normalised degree, is equivariant to that relabelling. On the hop field the
evaluation instances of the main grids are therefore copies of the training instances, and the
training error equals the test error exactly (Table~\ref{tab:fit}); the hop-field task on the
torus is a fitting problem on one field, not a generalisation problem. The delay field depends on
the link lengths, which vary with the snapshot, and differs between instances. On the seam-cut
graph the destination's position relative to the seam changes the hop field, and the instances
differ on both tasks (Section~\ref{sec:seam}).

\section{Protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

\subsection{Parameter matching and the arms}

The operators share depth, width, weight shapes and training loop, so a difference in error is
attributable to the operator and not to capacity. Two capacities are used: width 16 with three
layers, the default of the released code of the preliminary version (\numReleasedParams{}
parameters including the $t_{\ell}$), and width 32 with four layers, which the text of the
preliminary version describes and its released results were produced with
(\numSubmittedParams{} parameters). Adam~\cite{adam} at learning rate 0.01, eight training and six
evaluation instances per seed, and full-batch updates are used throughout. The six evaluation
instances of a seed are drawn with that seed's own generator, so the ten seeds of a cell evaluate
on sixty distinct snapshot-destination pairs; seed-to-seed dispersion therefore includes
instance-to-instance variation.

Four quantities are arms rather than fixed choices.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Parameterisation of $t$.} A pilot run under the released parameterisation showed the
  learned $t_{\ell}$ going negative in the second and third layers, where
  $\mathrm{relu}(t_{\ell})=0$ turns the propagator into the identity and stops its gradient, so
  that a model reported as three or four layers of spectral propagation was propagating in one.
  The softplus parameterisation removes this; the relu parameterisation is kept as a control so
  that the preliminary configuration is one of the arms.
\item \textbf{Initialisation of $t$.} $t_{0}\in\{0.5, 2\}$, since the same pilot showed the
  learned reach, and with it the operator ranking, depending on where $t$ starts.
\item \textbf{Capacity.} The two configurations above.
\item \textbf{Budget.} 200 epochs, the preliminary value, held fixed across scales; and 1000
  epochs for the second grid.
\item \textbf{Graph.} The torus of the main grids, and the seam-cut graph, run at 1000 epochs in
  all four $t$ arms (Section~\ref{sec:seam}).
\end{itemize}
Grid 1 runs both capacities, all four $t$ arms, four shells, two tasks, three operators and ten
seeds at 200 epochs (\numGridTwelveRuns{} runs). Grid 2 runs the width-32 configuration at 1000
epochs on the three smaller shells with \numGridThreeArms{} $t$ arms (\numGridThreeRuns{} runs).
With the fixed-reach baselines below (\numFixedRunsTwelve{} runs at 200 epochs and
\numFixedRunsThree{} at 1000), the two main grids comprise \numTotalRuns{} training runs; the
baseline ladder, the seam-cut arm and the precision check of Section~\ref{sec:collapse} add
\numLadderRuns{}, \numSeamRuns{} and \numPrecRuns{}. All runs were executed on one machine, one
GPU per arm, with the eigendecomposition of each instance cached in single precision and shared,
so that every arm sees bit-identical eigenpairs. Whether single precision is adequate is not
settled by that identity; it is tested directly by rerunning one cell, and then every certified
cell, entirely in double precision (Section~\ref{sec:collapse}). Together with the random-cut
control of Section~\ref{sec:seam} these add \numPsixfourRuns{} and \numRcutRuns{} runs, and the
seam-cut repeat of Grid~1 \numSeamGridOneRuns{}.

\paragraph{Fixed-reach decoupled baselines, and their measured reach}
A comparison of the three operators above confounds ``spectral'' with ``long-reaching'': the GCN
step reaches one hop per layer, the spectral kernels reach as far as their learned $t$ allows.
Two further operators, run at both budgets on the width-32 configuration, separate the two. Both
decouple propagation from transformation in the sense of SGC~\cite{sgc} and personalised-PageRank
propagation~\cite{appnp}, have fixed reach and no learned $t$, and use the same network:
\begin{equation}
\Phiop_{\mathrm{PPR}} = \alpha \sum_{k=0}^{K} (1-\alpha)^{k}\,\hat{\mathbf{A}}^{k},\quad
\alpha=\numPprAlpha,\ K=\numPprK;\qquad
\Phiop_{\mathrm{SGC}} = \hat{\mathbf{A}}^{K'},\quad K'=\numSgcK,
\label{eq:fixed}
\end{equation}
with $\hat{\mathbf{A}}$ the normalised adjacency of~\eqref{eq:gcn} and $\Phiop_{\mathrm{PPR}}$
renormalised to row sums of one. The values of $\alpha$, $K$ and $K'$ in~\eqref{eq:fixed} were
fixed by assertion in the first draft, on the reasoning that $1/\alpha$ and $K'$ hops are ``of the
order of'' the learned reach. That reasoning confuses the support of a propagator with where its
mass sits: on a lattice, $K'$ steps of a normalised adjacency place their mass at about
$\sqrt{K'}$ hops, not $K'$. Reach is therefore measured, not asserted. For one row of each
propagator, Table~\ref{tab:reach} reports the expected hop distance from the source under the
row's probability mass, per layer at the learned $t$ of Table~\ref{tab:tlearned} for the two
kernels and composed over the four layers. At 1584 satellites the heat kernel's layers reach
\numReachHeatLayerOneShellOne{} to \numReachHeatMeanShellOne{} hops on average and the walk
kernel's up to \numReachWalkLayerFourShellOne{}, while the baselines of~\eqref{eq:fixed} reach
\numReachPprRoundOneShellOne{} (PPR) and \numReachSgcEightShellOne{} (SGC) hops per layer: the
first-draft baselines reached about half as far as the kernels they were said to match, and the
truncation at $K=\numPprK$ steps caps the PPR reach whatever $\alpha$ says. The comparison is
therefore run over a ladder that brackets the learned reach on every shell: SGC with
$K'\in\{2,8,32,64\}$ and PPR computed exactly, $\alpha(\mathbf{I}-(1-\alpha)\hat{\mathbf{A}})^{-1}$,
with $\alpha\in\{0.2,0.05,0.02,0.01\}$, whose per-layer reach runs from 1.3 to

\subsection{Reading rules, declared before the runs, with their operating characteristics}
\label{sec:rules}

Six rules govern how a cell, one (task, shell, arm) combination, is read. The first five were
fixed before the first grid was launched, and two of them were tightened after the first runs
exposed a gap; both changes are dated in the repository and stated here. The sixth was added
after external review of a draft showed that the fourth, as stated, had a false-positive rate the
draft never computed, and its unit of inference was changed after a second review showed that
the first form of the rule had, by construction, almost no power; both forms are reported.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Constant baseline.} Every cell reports, on every metric it is scored on, the error
  of the predictor that outputs the mean of the target, in the tables themselves. Whether an
  operator beats it is a result, never a filter.
\item \textbf{Pathology.} A run is excluded only for a training pathology: a non-finite loss, or
  a loss that rises by more than 1\% between successive thirds of training. Excluded runs are
  counted next to every number they affect, and Section~\ref{sec:collapse} reports whether each
  excluded run had left the constant solution before collapsing, since exclusion after collapse
  removes non-escaping runs preferentially. A run whose loss is flat from the start because it
  sits on the constant solution is a \emph{plateau}, a legitimate outcome, and is kept. The first
  draft of this rule required a 10\% loss drop and would have discarded plateaus as failures; it
  was corrected when the first grid produced them.
\item \textbf{Floor.} Let $\bar{e}_{\mathrm{op}}$ be the mean MAE of an operator over its valid
  seeds in a cell and $\bar{e}_{0}$ that of the constant predictor. The cell's gain is
  \begin{equation}
  g = \frac{\bar{e}_{0} - \min_{\mathrm{op}} \bar{e}_{\mathrm{op}}}{\bar{e}_{0}},
  \label{eq:gain}
  \end{equation}
  and the cell is \emph{rankable} only if $g \geq 0.05$. This threshold was chosen after the
  first completed cells, all on the 4400-satellite shell, showed the three operators' means
  within \numLargeSpreadMax{} of one another and of the constant baseline while the majority rule
  below still declared a winner. Because the value was set with that knowledge, every statement
  that depends on it is repeated at $g\in\{0.01, 0.02, 0.05, 0.10\}$ in Section~\ref{sec:sens}.
\item \textbf{Seed majority.} Within a rankable cell, with $S$ the set of seeds valid for both
  spectral operators and $e_{\mathrm{op},s}$ the MAE of seed $s$, the heat kernel wins the
  majority if
  \begin{equation}
  \bar{e}_{\mathrm{heat}} < \bar{e}_{\mathrm{walk}}
  \quad\text{and}\quad
  \bigl|\{ s \in S : e_{\mathrm{heat},s} < e_{\mathrm{walk},s} \}\bigr| \geq 7,
  \label{eq:win}
  \end{equation}
  and symmetrically for the walk kernel; otherwise the cell is a tie, and fewer than seven valid
  seed pairs leaves it undecided. This is the rule of the preliminary protocol, and it is kept so
  that the preliminary verdicts can be reproduced. Its operating characteristics are the point:
  under the null of exchangeable operators, $P(X\geq 7 \mid n=10, p=\tfrac12) = 0.172$, so a
  winner is declared in 34.4\% of null cells, and the two-sided sign-test $p$-values behind a
  7:3, 8:2 and 8:1 majority are 0.344, 0.109 and 0.039. A majority verdict is therefore reported
  beside, never instead of, the tests of rule~6, and every table that prints a verdict also
  prints which operator led in mean, so that a ``tie'' cannot hide a lead.
\item \textbf{Robustness across arms.} A majority verdict at a (task, shell) is reported as
  robust only if it is the same in every $t$ arm run at that budget; like rule~4, this is
  descriptive, kept so that the preliminary protocol's verdicts can be reproduced, and no claim
  of this paper rests on it. Grid 2 runs \numGridThreeArms{} arms, and the number is stated
  wherever the rule is applied. Section~\ref{sec:seam} states explicitly where its one certified
  result stands under this rule.
\item \textbf{Inference.} The unit of inference is the (task, shell) pair, of which there are
  six at the larger budget. For each seed $s$, the per-seed difference
  $d_{s}=e_{\mathrm{heat},s}-e_{\mathrm{walk},s}$ is averaged over the $t$ arms in which seed $s$
  is valid for both kernels, giving one paired difference per seed; the ten values are summarised
  by their mean with a 95\% bootstrap confidence interval, by the exact two-sided sign test and by
  the exact two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test, and the Wilcoxon $p$-values are Holm-corrected
  over the six pairs of the same metric. A difference between the two kernels is claimed at a
  (task, shell) only where the corrected $p$ is below 0.05. The same statistics are also reported
  for every arm-level cell, with Holm correction over the \numGthreeArmCells{} cells, as the
  first draft did; these per-cell tests are descriptive, for the reason given next. The tests were
  checked against an independent implementation on random data and agree to nine decimal places.
\end{enumerate}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:cell} states rules 3 to 6 in the order in which the released code
(\texttt{analysis/rules.py}: \texttt{cell}, \texttt{summarise}, \texttt{pooled\_stats},
\texttt{holm}, \texttt{mde\_wilcoxon}) applies them to one (task, shell) pair; every table of
Section~\ref{sec:results} is one call of it per pair, and the exclusion counts it returns are
the ones printed in parentheses.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{Reading one (task, shell) pair (rules 3 to 6 of Section~\ref{sec:protocol})}
\label{alg:cell}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require per-seed MAE $e_{\mathrm{op},s,a}$ and constant-predictor MAE $e_{0,s,a}$ for operators
  $\mathrm{op}\in\{\mathrm{gcn},\mathrm{heat},\mathrm{walk}\}$, seeds $s\in\{1,\dots,10\}$,
  $t$ arms $a\in\mathcal{A}$; training-loss trajectories; the Holm family size $m=6$
\Ensure per-arm gain $g_{a}$, majority verdict $v_{a}$, pooled difference $\bar d$ with 95\%
  CI, corrected $p_{\mathrm{Holm}}$, minimum detectable effect, exclusion counts
\ForAll{runs $(\mathrm{op},s,a)$}                                       \Comment{rule 3}
  \State mark the run \emph{pathological} if its loss is non-finite or rises by more than
    1\% between successive thirds; a flat loss is a plateau and is kept
\EndFor
\ForAll{arms $a\in\mathcal{A}$}                                          \Comment{rules 4 and 5}
  \State $\bar e_{\mathrm{op}} \gets$ mean of $e_{\mathrm{op},s,a}$ over non-pathological seeds;
    $\bar e_{0} \gets$ mean of $e_{0,s,a}$ over all runs of the cell
  \State $g_{a} \gets$ gain of Eq.~\eqref{eq:gain}; if $g_{a}<0.05$ the cell is not rankable
  \State $S_{a} \gets$ seeds valid for both heat and walk; $v_{a} \gets$ verdict of
    Eq.~\eqref{eq:win} on $S_{a}$, undecided if $|S_{a}|<7$; record the leader in mean
\EndFor
\ForAll{seeds $s$}                                                        \Comment{rule 6}
  \State $d_{s} \gets$ mean over $\{a : s\in S_{a}\}$ of $e_{\mathrm{heat},s,a}-e_{\mathrm{walk},s,a}$
\EndFor
\State $\bar d \gets$ mean of $d_{s}$; CI from 2000 bootstrap resamples of $\{d_{s}\}$;
  vote $\gets (|\{d_{s}<0\}| : |\{d_{s}>0\}|)$
\State $p \gets$ exact two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test on $\{d_{s}\}$; $p_{\mathrm{sign}}
  \gets$ exact sign test on the vote
\State $p_{\mathrm{Holm}} \gets$ Holm correction of $p$ over the $m$ pairs of the same graph and metric
\State MDE $\gets$ smallest $|\bar d|$ detected with power 0.8 at the corrected threshold
  $0.05/m$, by simulation from the sample dispersion of $\{d_{s}\}$
\State \Return a difference is \emph{certified} at the pair only if $p_{\mathrm{Holm}}<0.05$;
  otherwise report $\bar d$, the CI and the MDE as the resolution of the design
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Operating characteristics of rule~6}
The exact Wilcoxon test on $n$ non-zero pairs cannot return a two-sided $p$ below $2/2^{n}$, and
Holm over $m$ tests requires the smallest $p$ to fall below $0.05/m$. Table~\ref{tab:power} applies
this to the arm-level cells of grid~2: with $m=\numGthreeArmCells$ the threshold is
$\numHolmThreshold$, which $n=9$ pairs cannot reach ($2/2^{9}=\numPowerMinPNine$) and $n=10$ pairs
reach only on a unanimous $10{:}0$ split ($\numPowerMinPTen$). Of the \numGthreeArmCells{} cells,
\numPowerCannotReject{} have nine or fewer valid pairs after exclusions and cannot reject at any
effect size, and the remaining \numPowerNeedSweep{} require every seed to agree. A ``0 of
\numGthreeArmCells{}'' under that rule is close to a property of the design, which is why the
first draft's headline, which reported exactly that, was withdrawn. The table also gives, for
each $|S|$, the minimum detectable effect (MDE) at the uncorrected 0.05 level and 80\% power,
computed by simulation from each cell's own seed dispersion. Pooling over arms gives every
(task, shell) pair its full ten seeds and reduces $m$ to 6, so the threshold becomes
$\numPooledThreshold$ and a $10{:}0$ or a favourable $9{:}1$ split can reject. The MDE of the
pooled test at the corrected level is reported beside every pooled result
(Table~\ref{tab:pooled}); it is what the design can resolve, and where the observed difference is
smaller than it the correct statement is that the design cannot certify a difference, not that
none exists. The counts $|S|$ that this table is computed from are themselves not fixed by the design:
Section~\ref{sec:collapse} shows that which runs are excluded depends on the floating-point
precision, so that in the one cell run in both precisions $|S|$ is \numPrecSThirtyTwo{} in single
and \numPrecSSixtyFour{} in double precision with different seeds excluded, and
Section~\ref{sec:collapse} reports whether the certified results survive a change of precision.
Dependence among the tests is stated rather than assumed away: the four arms at a
(task, shell) share instances, seeds and eigenbases, which is why they are pooled into one test,
and the two tasks of a shell share graphs and seeds, so the six pooled tests are not independent.
Holm's procedure controls the family-wise error rate under arbitrary dependence, so the correction
remains valid; it is conservative, not invalid, under this structure.

\subsection{The seam-cut graph}
\label{sec:seam}

Every run above is on the torus. The seam-cut graph removes the inter-plane links between the
last and the first plane, so that $2S$ satellites have degree three, the graph is no longer
vertex-transitive, and a destination's hop field depends on where it sits relative to the seam.
It is the graph both versions of this study said they used. Grid~2 is repeated on it at 1000
epochs, submitted capacity, in \numSeamArms{} $t$ arms with the three operators
(\numSeamCoreRuns{} runs, of which \numSeamPathology{}, \numSeamPathologyPct\%, are excluded under

same six rules. Table~\ref{tab:seam} sets the softplus, $t_{0}=0.5$ arm on the two graphs side by

on the far-node metric, and Tables~\ref{tab:seamgrid} and~\ref{tab:seamgridinf} in the appendix
every arm.

separate by up to \numSeamFitMaxGap{}, and the GCN's seed dispersion, which is
\numGthreeGcnSdMax{} or less in every cell of grid~2 on the torus, reaches \numSeamGcnSdMax{}. Second, the

and \numPrecWalkDelta{} (walk), which is the distance between the escaped and the constant
solution: the same seed, with the same initial weights and the same instances, escapes in one

this scale the precision acts as one more random seed. It does not affect all arms alike, as the
first draft's threat statement assumed; it affects each run separately, and the seed-level
bimodality of Section~\ref{sec:hidden} is its visible form.

\subsection{Cost, and the scale at which the method is feasible}
\label{sec:cost}

Table~\ref{tab:cost} reports the median wall-clock time per run on one consumer GPU with 24~GB
of memory, with the eigendecomposition of each instance cached. The spectral operators build an
$N\times N$ propagator per layer per instance per epoch from the eigenbasis, an $O(N^{3})$ product
that dominates at scale; the walk kernel builds two such products, for the real and imaginary
parts, and squares them. On the 4400-satellite shell at 200 epochs a walk run takes
\numSecsWalkLarge{}~s against \numSecsHeatLarge{}~s for the heat kernel and \numSecsGcnLarge{}~s
for the GCN, whose propagator is sparse. On CPU, before caching, the same cell took
\numCpuSecsLarge{}~s, which is why the preliminary version stopped at 1584 satellites. As
implemented, with dense $N\times N$ single-precision matrices for the eigenbasis, the propagator
and their gradients, the method fits in 24~GB up to roughly $N=\numFeasibleNGpuMem{}$ nodes;
beyond that it needs a factorised or sparse propagator, which is a different implementation. The
mega-constellations of the introduction lie at the edge of that range and beyond it. For a
comparison across scales, the cost of the operator is part of what is being compared.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What was tested, and what was not}
The object of this audit is the transition-probability kernel~\eqref{eq:walk}. It is a real
nonnegative row-stochastic matrix, exactly as the heat kernel is, and applying it to features
damps and mixes them as a diffusion step does; what it inherits from the walk is the shape of its
rows, an oscillatory profile whose width grows linearly with $t$ rather than as a square root
(Figure~\ref{fig:spreading}). Once $t$ is a trained parameter, the heat kernel reaches as far by
learning a larger $t$, which the right panel of Figure~\ref{fig:tlearned} and
Table~\ref{tab:reach} show it does, in the first layer and in proportion to the shell. What the
paired tests find has to be stated in two halves. Where the design has power, on the
264-satellite shell, the heat kernel is better on both tasks on both graphs
(Tables~\ref{tab:pooled} and~\ref{tab:seampooled}); \numCIExclZeroSmall{} of the
\numCIExclZero{} per-cell intervals that exclude zero are on that shell, and they point the same
way. On the larger shells of the torus the tests certify nothing, and the reason is stated in
MAE units: the minimum detectable effect there is \numPooledMdeLargeMin{} to
\numPooledMdeMax{}, and the observed differences are smaller. So is the difference the audit set
out to test: at 1584 satellites on the hop field the resolution is \numPooledHopsShellOneMde{}
and the preliminary claim was \numPrelimDiff{}, \numMdeRatioHopsShellOne{} times finer, so
\numTotalRuns{} runs on the torus could establish the budget floor, the arm dependence and the
264-satellite ranking, and could not have confirmed or refuted the preliminary claim at its own
scale. That is absence of evidence, not evidence of absence, for the observed and for the
audited difference alike, and the first draft's sentence that the kernels ``are
indistinguishable'' overstated a null in the same way that the preliminary version overstated a
difference. On the seam-cut graph the walk kernel takes the 1584-satellite hop field at the
corrected level on MAE, on nine seeds, and not on the far-node metric
(Section~\ref{sec:seam}); the direction is real on that graph and that metric, and it is no
broader than that. The
argument of CTQW-GNN~\cite{ctqwgnn} for the unitary propagator in feature space, which damps no
frequency and preserves feature norm, is a different object and is neither confirmed nor refuted
here.

\paragraph{What the floor means for learned propagation on constellations}
A budget that suffices on a 264-satellite shell does not on a 1584-satellite one, and the failure
is silent: the run reports a finite, decreasing loss and an error that looks like a number.
Carrying the constant baseline beside every result is necessary and not sufficient: at 200
epochs the gains over it on the large shells were positive in every cell
(Table~\ref{tab:floor}), and what exposed the floor was a threshold on the gain, whose value this
study set with the 4400-satellite cells in view (Section~\ref{sec:rules}). The operative safeguard
is a gain threshold fixed and justified before the runs, and a budget that is shown to clear it
on every shell; the present study can only report that its own threshold was post hoc and that
its conclusions hold at four values of it.

\paragraph{What the seed-level bimodality and the vote noise mean}
The split in Figure~\ref{fig:arms}, with escape rates of \numEscapeHeatPct\% and
\numEscapeWalkPct\% ($p=\numEscapePz$), means that at the preliminary budget a majority verdict
counts which seeds happened to escape. At the larger budget the majority statistic still moves by
up to \numSwapMaxMoved{} seeds between two correlated metrics on the same runs
(Section~\ref{sec:swap}), which is the margin by which most of its verdicts were declared. The
preliminary version's \numPrelimSeeds{}-seed result at 1584 satellites sits inside the range that
this bimodality produces on the torus, where ten seeds split \numPooledHopsShellOneWins{}; on
the seam-cut graph the same cell splits \numSeamPooledHopsShellOneWins{} in the preliminary
direction. The bimodality itself is not a property of the seeds: Section~\ref{sec:collapse}
shows that the precision of the arithmetic moves a seed between the two modes, so the split is
a property of the training dynamics at this scale, and the number of seeds only measures it.

\paragraph{Consequences for reporting}
These are not new rules, and they are not the contribution; they are what this audit needed to
avoid repeating its own preliminary version, and two of them are what its own first draft needed
and lacked. Carry the constant predictor beside every result, on every metric. Let the budget grow
with the graph, or show that it need not. Treat any learned reach parameter's parameterisation and
initialisation as an arm. Measure the reach of a ``reach-matched'' baseline. Use a paired test
with a multiplicity correction, state the false-positive rate of any vote rule reported beside
it, and state the minimum effect the corrected test can detect, since a null from a test that
could not reject is not a finding. Count collapsed runs and report whether they had escaped. Check
that the graph the code builds is the graph the text describes, by a property the text can be
wrong about, such as the degree sequence.

\paragraph{What would change the verdict}
A propagator applied in feature space, unitary rather than stochastic, would test the argument of
CTQW-GNN~\cite{ctqwgnn} directly; the wave-type models PDE-GCN~\cite{pdegcn} and graph-coupled
oscillator networks~\cite{graphcon} are the classical form of the same argument and were not run.
A learning-rate schedule would reduce the collapse rate of Section~\ref{sec:collapse}, and with
it the chaotic sensitivity to precision, and might decide cells that are now undecided; more
seeds would lower the minimum detectable effect of the pooled tests, which at 1584 satellites on
the hop field is \numPooledHopsShellOneMde{} with ten. Other link patterns than the torus and the
seam cut, and the time-varying links of a real constellation, would say how far the
seam-cut result of Section~\ref{sec:seam} generalises. A task with load, in which the field to be predicted depends
on traffic and not only on geometry, and a time-varying evaluation that exercises the link
churn the introduction invokes, would say whether any operator ranking survives contact with a
network function. None of these is a small addition, and the present paper claims nothing about
them.

\section{Threats to validity}
\label{sec:threats}

\textbf{The two largest shells are synthetic, and all graphs are static.} The 3168- and
4400-satellite graphs scale the Starlink shell-1 geometry and are not deployed constellations;
they serve to grow the diameter. Every instance is an independent snapshot, so the time-varying
property that motivates learned propagation on these graphs is never exercised.

\textbf{The tasks are node fields, not network functions.} Hop and delay fields isolate
propagation from load, which is what the comparison needs, at the cost of distance from any
routing or traffic-engineering objective. The findings bear on the evaluation of propagation
operators, not on the routing or resource-management systems that might use them.

\textbf{The floor threshold was chosen with the data in view.} Rule~3's value was set after the
4400-satellite cells were seen, as Section~\ref{sec:rules} states; Table~\ref{tab:sens} reports
every dependent count at four values of it.

\textbf{The majority rule has a 34.4\% null false-positive rate, and the per-cell test has
almost no power.} The majority rule is kept only so that the preliminary verdicts are
reproducible, and no conclusion rests on it. The per-cell Holm-corrected Wilcoxon test that the
first draft stood on cannot reject in \numPowerCannotReject{} of \numGthreeArmCells{} cells at
any effect size (Table~\ref{tab:power}); it is reported as description. The pooled test the
paper now stands on has ten seeds per test and a minimum detectable effect that is stated beside
every result; on the large shells it is \numPooledMdeLargeMin{} to \numPooledMdeMax{} in MAE
units, which is coarse.

\textbf{The baseline ladder is a ladder, not a tuning.} $\alpha$ and $K'$ take four values each,
chosen to bracket the measured reach of the kernels, and the best rung is selected on the same
seeds the comparison is scored on, which favours the baselines. A finer search might find a
better fixed reach; it might also find a worse one, since the ladder's top rung already
over-smooths the small shell. The rungs are unequally censored (\numLadderExclMinPct\% to
\numLadderExclMaxPct\% excluded), and the minimum over rungs leans towards the more censored
ones, which flatters the baselines further.

\IfFileExists{../figures/out/tab-randomcut.tex}{\textbf{The random-cut control is not certified,
and it does not identify the mechanism.} The control of Section~\ref{sec:seam} is one cell on
one graph: its $\bar d$ of \numRcutRandomcutD{} is below its corrected resolution of
\numRcutRandomcutMde, its $p$ of \numRcutRandomcutPW{} is uncorrected because the cell has no
family, the walk kernel leads in \numRcutRandomcutWalkArms{} of its \numRcutRandomcutArms{} arms
where it leads in all four on the seam-cut graph, and its training-to-test separation
(\numRcutFitMaxGap) matches the torus (\numFitMaxGapHopsThree), not the seam-cut graph
(\numSeamFitMaxGap). It shows that the seam is not necessary for the direction of the reversal;
it does not certify the reversal on its own graph, and it contradicts the held-out-content
account of why the cell moves. The grid-1 comparison of Table~\ref{tab:seamfloor} covers
\numSeamGridOneArms{} of the four $t$ arms.

}{}\textbf{Exclusion is differential.} Rule~2 removes runs after they have collapsed to the constant
solution, so it removes non-escapers preferentially, and the GCN loses more runs than the spectral
kernels (Table~\ref{tab:collapse}). Means are therefore optimistic, most for the GCN.

\textbf{One learning rate, one optimiser, six evaluation instances per seed.} The collapse rate is
specific to Adam at 0.01 for 1000 epochs. Each seed evaluates on its own six instances, so the
sixty instances of a cell are distinct, but instance-level dispersion within a seed is not
reported.

\textbf{The arithmetic is single precision, and the trajectories are chaotic.} Every arm
shares one single-precision eigendecomposition per instance. Section~\ref{sec:collapse} shows
that rerunning a cell in double precision moves individual seeds between the escaped and the
collapsed mode and changes the sign of the cell's mean paired difference. The comparison between
kernels within a precision is fair, since both see the same rounding; but a single run's outcome
is not reproducible across precisions, and a result that rests on a handful of seeds, including
the preliminary version's, is a sample from this chaos. \IfFileExists{../figures/out/tab-precision-certified.tex}{Section~\ref{sec:collapse} reruns the three
certified cells in double precision and reports which certifications hold in both.}{The three
certified cells were not rerun in double precision in this version, so their certification rests
on one arithmetic path.}

\textbf{Several references are preprints.} CTQW-GNN~\cite{ctqwgnn}, CTQWformer~\cite{ctqwformer}
and the LEO graph-learning work of Gu et al.~\cite{deepladu} are unrefereed at the time of
writing, and GraphQWalk~\cite{graphqwalk} is a recent journal article. The argument this audit
declines to test rests on the first of these; the audit itself rests on the preliminary version,
which is not in the refereed record either.

\textbf{The preliminary artifact does not reproduce its own headline, and both versions
misdescribed their graph.} The released results of the preliminary version record
\numPrelimParams{} parameters for the 1584-satellite experiment, which corresponds to the
width-32, four-layer network its text describes, while the released code defaults to width 16
and three layers (\numReleasedParams{} parameters) and contains no script for that experiment.
Both the preliminary version and the first drafts of this paper state that the counter-rotating
seam is cut. In the generator the flag \texttt{seam=True} keeps the seam links, and every run of
the two main grids was made with it; a docstring written for this study read the flag the other
way, and the text copied the docstring. The error was found by reading Table~\ref{tab:fit}, where
training and test error coincide, and confirmed by the degree sequence: every node of every
main-grid snapshot has degree four. The main grids are therefore on the torus, described as such
throughout this version, and the seam-cut graph is run as its own arm
(Section~\ref{sec:seam}). The preliminary version's 1584-satellite claim was, in the event, made on
the torus and recovered here on the seam-cut graph; neither its text nor its authors knew which
graph it was on.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We audited a preliminary result of our own: that the transition-probability kernel of a
continuous-time quantum walk beats the heat kernel on a 1584-satellite ISL graph,
\numPrelimWalkMAE{} against \numPrelimHeatMAE{} from \numPrelimSeeds{} seeds, and did so
increasingly with scale. What the audit establishes is listed here with the table each statement
is read from, and nothing is claimed that is not in one of them.
\begin{itemize}
\item Under the preliminary budget the shells of 1584 satellites and more are a floor on the
  torus: the best operator gains at most \numGainLargeMax\% over the constant predictor in any
  arm at either capacity (Table~\ref{tab:floor}).\IfFileExists{../figures/out/tab-seam-floor.tex}{
  On the seam-cut graph, in \numSeamGridOneArms{} of the four arms, \numSeamGridOneFloorLarge{} of
  \numSeamGridOneLargeCells{} large-shell cells are below the floor and one, the 1584-satellite hop
  cell, reaches \numSeamGainLargeMax\%; the gains at 1584 satellites are
  \numSeamGridOneRatioMin{} to \numSeamGridOneRatioMax{} times the torus's in the same arms
  (Table~\ref{tab:seamfloor}), so the budget sets the floor on both graphs and the link pattern
  sets the depth below it.}{ The seam-cut graph was not run at this budget.}
\item At the one shell where learning occurs at that budget, the ranking follows the
  parameterisation and initialisation of $t$ through a seed-level bimodality with escape rates of
  \numEscapeHeatPct\% and \numEscapeWalkPct\% (Tables~\ref{tab:s264} and~\ref{tab:escape}).
\item With five times the budget, on the torus, the heat kernel is better on the 264-satellite
  shell on both tasks (Table~\ref{tab:pooled}): two dependent tests on one shell, both at the
  smallest corrected $p$ the design can return, one of them on a task that has no held-out
  content on that graph (Table~\ref{tab:fit}). On the larger shells the design resolves
  \numPooledMdeLargeMin{} to \numPooledMdeMax{} (Table~\ref{tab:pooled}); at 1584 satellites on
  the hop field that is \numMdeRatioHopsShellOne{} times the preliminary difference, so the
  torus grid is silent on the claim it audited. The per-cell test of the previous draft could not
  have rejected in \numPowerCannotReject{} of \numGthreeArmCells{} cells (Table~\ref{tab:power}).
\item On the seam-cut graph, the graph both versions described and neither had run, the walk
  kernel takes the 1584-satellite hop cell on MAE at the corrected level
  (Table~\ref{tab:seampooled}), on nine seeds, surviving three of four ways of restoring the
  tenth; on the far-node metric nothing is certified on that graph
  (Table~\ref{tab:seampooledaurc}). The other five (task, shell) pairs read as on the
  torus.\IfFileExists{../figures/out/tab-randomcut.tex}{ On a control that removes as many links
  at random positions the same cell gives \numRcutRandomcutWins{} with
  $\bar d=\numRcutRandomcutD$, the sign of the seam cut but below the control's own corrected
  resolution of \numRcutRandomcutMde{} and uncorrected (Table~\ref{tab:randomcut}): the seam is
  not necessary for the direction, the control certifies nothing, and the held-out-content
  mechanism is contradicted by the control's training-to-test separation.}{}
\item Against a fixed reach tuned over a measured ladder, a learned $t$ in its best arm is ahead
  by \numLadderGapBestPctHopsShellOne\% at 1584 satellites on the hop field and behind in its
  worst arm (Table~\ref{tab:ladder}); the first-draft baselines reached half as far as the
  kernels they were said to match (Table~\ref{tab:reach}).
\item Which long runs collapse to the constant solution changes with the floating-point
  precision (Table~\ref{tab:precision})\IfFileExists{../figures/out/tab-precision-certified.tex}{;
  of the three certified cells rerun in double precision, the 264-satellite hop and delay cells
  give \numPsixfourHopsSmallWins{} and \numPsixfourDelaySmallWins{} and the seam-cut 1584-satellite
  cell \numPsixfourSeamHopsShellOneWins{}: all three hold (Table~\ref{tab:precisioncertified})}{; the certified
  cells were not rerun}.
\end{itemize}
The two kernels are the same kind of object, a real row-stochastic propagator with a learned
reach, and on these graphs their ranking is decided by the budget, the parameterisation of $t$,
and the change from the torus to the seam-cut graph before it is decided by the kernel. What the audit adds beyond the
correction of its own report is measured: a silent budget floor that scales with the graph, a
propagation-time parameterisation that decides rankings at small budgets, a vote statistic whose
noise amplitude is two to three seeds, a per-cell corrected test that could not have rejected in
three quarters of its cells, a ``reach-matched'' baseline that reached half as far as the
kernels it was matched to, a misread flag that put every main-grid run on a graph no
constellation realises, and a training dynamics chaotic enough that the arithmetic's precision
decides which seeds collapse. Each is a reason to distrust a small-seed operator comparison on
constellation graphs, including the one we published first and the one we drafted second.
