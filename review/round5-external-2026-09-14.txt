Reviewer Report (Critical Assessment)

Manuscript: "Training Budget, Time Parameterisation and Link Pattern Decide the Ranking of Propagation Operators on LEO Inter-Satellite-Link Graphs" File: New folder (41)/main (208).pdf — 41 pages, single author Status: Fourth revision. Diff against New folder (37): 260 added / 218 deleted lines. New material: Table 7 expanded from two arms to four (960 runs), dual-level minimum detectable effects and per-arm leader counts in Table 19, a rewritten control passage in §5.5, a dedicated threats entry, and an explanation of the control's GCN dispersion.

Manuscript Summary (Focus: Claims vs. Support)

All thirteen requirements of the previous round are discharged, and the most consequential of them changed what the paper says. The random-cut control is now read under rule 6 like every other cell and found to certify nothing — d̄ = +0.0300 against a corrected resolution of 0.061 — so the abstract now reads "a control that removes as many links at random positions moves the cell in the same direction but below its own resolution, so the seam is not necessary for the direction and the mechanism is not identified." The held-out-content account is withdrawn on the strength of the paper's own measurement (0.000 on the torus, 0.001 on the random cut, 0.009 on the seam cut): "on the variable nominated as the mechanism the control sits with the torus while on the outcome it sits with the seam cut." Table 19's apparently contradictory minimum detectable effects are resolved as a labelling omission — both levels are now printed, 0.087 (0.05/6) and 0.057 (0.05) — and rule 5 is applied to the control, which fails it 3 of 4 arms where the seam cut passes 4 of 4. Grid 1 now runs on the seam-cut graph in all four arms, and §5.1 and §8's floor bullet report the one cell that clears the threshold instead of asserting graph-independence.

This is the first version of this manuscript in which I can find no claim that the table it cites does not support. Two items remain, both bookkeeping: the Data and Code Availability section still reports 1350 auxiliary runs when §4.1 now enumerates 2670, and §6 discusses the seam-cut result without the control that qualifies it.

Detailed Critical Evaluation

1. Novelty, Significance, and Clarity of Objectives

Assessment: Acceptable

Critique. The title's third clause is now given a meaning the evidence supports. §5.5 states it explicitly: "The 'link pattern' of the title refers to the one change of graph this paper certifies, from the torus to the seam-cut graph; whether every departure from vertex-transitivity has the same effect is the question the control was run to answer, and it leaves it open." §8's closing sentence follows suit, replacing the general factor with "the change from the torus to the seam-cut graph." A title that names a factor whose mechanism is unidentified but whose effect is certified at one cell is defensible on those terms, and the paper now says so where a reader will meet it.

The framing of the torus's unreality, established in the previous round, is unchanged and remains correct: §1 states that all 2920 main-grid runs are on a graph "that no constellation realises" and that "every claim of this paper that is specific to LEO inter-satellite-link graphs rests on that arm alone."

Requirements for Rectification. None.

2. Methodological Soundness and Transparency

Assessment: Acceptable

Critique. Every methodological requirement is met, and three are met in ways that improve on what was asked.

The control is read under the paper's own rule. §5.5: "The control is read under rule 6 like every other cell, and under that rule it does not certify anything: its d̄ of +0.0300 is below the 0.061 the design resolves at the corrected level for this cell (Table 19 prints both the corrected and the uncorrected resolution), its Wilcoxon p of 0.049 is uncorrected because the control stands alone rather than in a family of six, and its interval [+0.0059, +0.0518] would not survive the correction the seam-cut result carries." All three asymmetries I raised are stated in one passage, and the sentence introducing it — "A control was run to separate them, and it separates them less than we had hoped" — is the right register for a control that failed.

Rule 5 is applied and reported in the table. Table 19's pooled lines now end with "walk leads 3/4 arms," "walk leads 4/4 arms," "walk leads 3/4 arms," and §5.5 names the dissenting arm ("the heat kernel leading the softplus, t₀ = 0.5 arm").

The MDE question was a labelling defect, not a numerical one. Table 19 now prints "MDE 0.087 (0.05/6), 0.057 (0.05)" and the corresponding pairs for the other two graphs. The previous version printed only the uncorrected value without marking it, which is what made it appear to contradict Tables 13 and 21; both values were always correct at their own level. The ratios behave as they should with the sample size (1.526 and 1.525 at n = 10, 1.412 at n = 9).

Grid 1 is now complete on the seam-cut graph. §4.1 records 960 runs across all four arms, and Table 7 carries all four.

The anomalous dispersion is explained. §5.5: the random-cut GCN "returns the same ten values in every arm by construction, and its seed dispersion of 0.0005 (range 0.0014) is below the three decimals printed, because on this graph the one-hop operator sits within 0.011 of the constant predictor in every seed and the random removal leaves little for the seed to move."

§7 has a dedicated entry. "The random-cut control is not certified, and it does not identify the mechanism," listing all four qualifications including the separation figures.

The one residual is an enumeration that is never summed. §4.1 lists the auxiliary runs across two sentences — "the baseline ladder, the seam-cut arm and the precision check of Section 5.7 add 480, 840 and 30 … Together with the random-cut control of Section 5.5 these add 240 and 120 runs, and the seam-cut repeat of Grid 1 960" — and gives no total. That is what allowed the error in criterion 3(a) to pass.

Requirements for Rectification.

State the auxiliary total in §4.1 (2670) and the grand total (5590) in one place, so that later sections have a figure to cite rather than to re-derive.

3. Data Integrity, Results Presentation, and Analysis

Assessment: Minor Weaknesses requiring significant rework

Critique. The tables reconcile throughout, including all the new material. Table 7's 24 large-shell cells carry 23 daggers and one clearing cell, exactly as §5.1 and §8 state. The arm-by-arm gains at 1584 satellites on the hop field — 4.6, 2.9, 5.9, 2.4 on the seam cut against 2.8, 1.6, 1.0, 1.1 on the torus — give ratios of 1.64, 1.81, 5.90 and 2.18, matching the stated "1.6 to 5.9 times". Table 19's per-arm entries still agree cell for cell with Tables 10, 12, A.30 and A.31; Table 25's double-precision reruns and their substituted Holm values are unchanged and correct.

The rewritten passages are accurate. §5.1 no longer asserts graph-independence: "the floor therefore holds on both graphs at 5%, but it is not equally deep … The budget sets the floor on both graphs; the link pattern sets how far below it the large shells sit, and on the seam-cut graph the 1584-satellite hop cell sits at the threshold rather than under it." §8's first bullet reports the exception rather than asserting a floor over it. And §5.5 now connects the precision result to the marginality argument where that argument is made: "the same cell rerun in double precision gives d̄ = +0.0293 on ten seeds, a change of a quarter from the arithmetic alone, so the effect cannot be placed against that threshold to two decimals … certified in both, by a margin that the precision moves."

One defect remains, and it is of the kind this revision added rather than inherited.

(a) The Data and Code Availability section reports a run total that predates this revision. It states that the repository holds "every result file of the 2920 main-grid and 1350 auxiliary runs." The auxiliary figure 1350 is exactly 480 + 840 + 30 — the ladder, the seam-cut grid 2 and the single-cell precision check, which is the set enumerated in the previous version. This round added 240 double-precision reruns of the certified cells, 120 for the random-cut control and 960 for the seam-cut repeat of Grid 1, all of which §4.1 lists. The auxiliary total is 2670 and the grand total 5590, not 4270. A reader auditing the released repository against the paper will find 1320 more result files than the paper says are there.

(b) §6 discusses the seam-cut result without the control. §6 states "On the seam-cut graph the walk kernel takes the 1584-satellite hop field at the corrected level on MAE, on nine seeds, and not on the far-node metric (Section 5.5); the direction is real on that graph and that metric, and it is no broader than that." That is correct as far as it goes, but §6 is the interpretive section and the control materially changes how the finding should be read — the seam is not necessary for the direction, and the mechanism is unidentified. §5.5, §7 and §8 all carry it; §6 does not, so a reader who reads the discussion for the paper's interpretation of its most consequential result gets it without the qualification.

Requirements for Rectification.

Update the Data and Code Availability figure to 2670 auxiliary runs (5590 total), and check that no other downstream total was left at its pre-revision value.

Add one sentence to §6's seam-cut paragraph recording that a random cut reproduces the direction below its own resolution and that the mechanism is not identified.

4. Discussion, Interpretation, and Conclusions

Assessment: Acceptable

Critique. §8 retains the table-keyed structure introduced in the previous round — "What the audit establishes is listed here with the table each statement is read from, and nothing is claimed that is not in one of them" — and every bullet now satisfies it. The seam-cut bullet carries the control in full: "the seam is not necessary for the direction, the control certifies nothing, and the held-out-content mechanism is contradicted by the control's training-to-test separation." The floor bullet scopes the 2.9% figure to the torus and reports the 5.9% seam-cut cell in the same sentence, which resolves the self-contradiction of the previous version.

§7's threats list has grown the entry it lacked and now covers, among the rest, the post-hoc floor threshold, the per-cell test's lack of power, the ladder's unequal censoring, the differential exclusion, the chaotic trajectories, and the control. §6's omission noted above is the only gap.

Requirements for Rectification. Covered by 3(2).

5. Literature Context and Contribution

Assessment: Acceptable

Critique. §2 is unchanged and remains adequate and honestly scoped, with preprint status disclosed at first mention and in §7, and the wave-type analogues named in §6 as the control family not run.

Requirements for Rectification. None.

6. Writing Quality, Precision, and Conciseness

Assessment: Acceptable

Critique. For the first time across four revisions, I could not find a summary-level sentence that the tables do not support. The abstract, §1's contribution bullets, §5.1, §5.5, §6, §7 and §8 all carry the qualifications their results sections attach, and the figures — which lagged in the previous round of the companion submission and in earlier rounds of this one — were regenerated with the text.

The manuscript is long (41 pages) and the auxiliary-run enumeration in §4.1 is the one place where the prose has become hard to follow: three run counts in one sentence, then three more in the next, with no total. That is a small cost of an unusually complete accounting, but it is also the proximate cause of the stale figure in criterion 3(a).

Requirements for Rectification.

Present the run accounting as a short table rather than two sentences, with the grand total stated once and cited everywhere else.

Incisive Critical Questions for the Authors

§4.1 enumerates 480 + 840 + 30 + 240 + 120 + 960 = 2670 auxiliary runs, while the Data and Code Availability section states 1350 — the figure from before this revision added the double-precision reruns, the random-cut control and the seam-cut repeat of Grid 1. Which total describes the released repository, and were any other derived counts left at their previous values?

§6 reads the seam-cut result as "real on that graph and that metric, and no broader than that," without the control that §5.5, §7 and §8 all attach to it. Is the discussion's interpretation of the paper's one certified graph effect intended to stand without the finding that a random cut reproduces the direction and that the mechanism is unidentified?

Table 7 shows the seam-cut gains at 264 satellites as 47.0, 24.5, 46.3, 26.5% against the torus's 34.6, 32.7, 40.3, 24.9% — higher in the two t₀ = 0.5 arms and lower in one t₀ = 2 arm. §5.1 draws its conclusion about depth from the large shells only. Does the 264-satellite pattern bear on the claim that the link pattern sets the depth, or is it arm-dependent in a way that should be said?

The random-cut control varies the removed links per snapshot while the seam cut removes a fixed set. Since the control's instances therefore differ from one another in a second way the seam cut's do not, does the 0.001 train–test separation measure the same quantity on both graphs, and could that account for its sitting with the torus?

Rule 5 now separates the two graphs (4 of 4 arms against 3 of 4), and the control's dissenting arm is softplus t₀ = 0.5 — the arm in which the seam cut shows its strongest result (0:8, p = 0.008). Is that coincidence, or does it bear on which feature of the graph the arm is sensitive to?

Concluding Remarks

Recommendation: Minor Revisions.

Across four rounds this manuscript has been asked, each time, to bring its summary sections into line with its tables, and each time the correction has arrived accompanied by a fresh claim in the newly written material. This revision breaks that pattern. Every one of the thirteen requirements is discharged, and the three that mattered were discharged against the author's own interest: the random-cut control is now read under rule 6 and found to certify nothing, the held-out-content mechanism is withdrawn because the paper's own separation figures contradict it, and the title's third factor is narrowed to the single change of graph the evidence certifies. "A control was run to separate them, and it separates them less than we had hoped" is a sentence very few authors write about an experiment they added in response to review. Alongside it, Grid 1 was extended to all four arms on the seam-cut graph, the apparently contradictory resolutions in Table 19 were resolved as a labelling omission with both levels now printed, rule 5 was applied to the control and reported in the table, the anomalous GCN dispersion was explained with a reason rather than a disclaimer, and the precision result was connected to the marginality argument it bears on. I re-derived Table 7's dagger count, the 1.6-to-5.9 ratio range, the MDE level ratios and Table 19's per-arm agreement with the appendix tables; all reconcile.

What remains is two items of bookkeeping. The Data and Code Availability section still reports 1350 auxiliary runs, the figure from before this round added 1320 more, so the paper understates its own released artifact by a quarter. And §6, alone among the interpretive sections, reads the seam-cut result without the control that qualifies it — a missing cross-reference rather than a missing argument, since §5.5, §7 and §8 all carry it. Neither requires an experiment, a re-analysis, or a number the author does not have; one is a corrected total and a run-accounting table, the other a sentence.

With those made I would recommend acceptance. The paper that results is a reproducibility audit that found a false result of its own, then a misread flag that put 2920 runs on a graph it did not intend to study, then a control that took away its explanation of the one result it recovered — and reported each of the three in its abstract. That is a more useful contribution than the finding it set out to defend.
