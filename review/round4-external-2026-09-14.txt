Reviewer Report (Critical Assessment)

Manuscript: "Training Budget, Time Parameterisation and Link Pattern Decide the Ranking of Propagation Operators on LEO Inter-Satellite-Link Graphs" File: New folder (37)/main (207).pdf — 40 pages (was 36), single author Status: Third revision. Diff against New folder (34): 589 added / 380 deleted lines. New material: Table 7 (grid 1 on the seam-cut graph), Table 19 (the 1584-satellite hop cell on three graphs including a random-cut control), Table 22 (far-node pooled tests on the seam-cut graph), Table 25 (the three certified cells rerun in double precision), and a seed-restoration sensitivity analysis in §5.5.

Manuscript Summary (Focus: Claims vs. Support)

Every requirement of the previous round was met, several with results that changed the paper. The torus's unreality now governs the abstract and §1 ("every claim of this paper that is specific to LEO inter-satellite-link graphs rests on that arm alone"); the 4.3× ratio between the design's resolution and the audited effect is stated where the null is claimed; the two certified torus cells are labelled as dependent tests at the design's minimum returnable p, one on a task with no held-out content; the seam-cut result now carries its metric-specificity, its nine seeds, and a four-way analysis of the missing tenth; all three certified cells were rerun in double precision and hold; grid 1 was run on the seam-cut graph. The demanded topology-versus-task control was also run, and it overturned the interpretation: a random cut reproduces the reversal, so the title now reads "Link Pattern" and the mechanism is attributed to lost vertex-transitivity rather than the seam.

That new control is where the manuscript now overreaches. Its effect, +0.0300, is below its own minimum detectable effect of 0.040 — the threshold the paper itself says means a difference cannot be certified; its p is uncorrected while the seam-cut result it is matched to is Holm-corrected; one of its four arms favours the other kernel; and on the mechanism variable the paper names, train/test separation, it sits at 0.001 against the seam cut's 0.009 and the torus's 0.000 — nine times closer to the graph whose behaviour it does not reproduce. Separately, Table 19 prints minimum detectable effects for two cells that contradict Tables 13 and 21 for the same cells (0.057 against 0.087; 0.017 against 0.024), and both of the paper's most-quoted figures depend on the larger values.

Detailed Critical Evaluation

1. Novelty, Significance, and Clarity of Objectives

Assessment: Acceptable

Critique. The restructuring requested last round has been carried out at the level of the title, the abstract and §1 rather than by a note. §1's third paragraph now opens "One fact about the graph governs how the rest of the paper must be read," states that all 2920 main-grid runs are on "a graph that no constellation realises," and draws the consequence: "every claim of this paper that is specific to LEO inter-satellite-link graphs rests on that arm alone." The abstract carries the same sentence. This is the correct disposition of a construction error that invalidates the setting of the bulk of the work, and it is made without diminishing the torus results, which remain legitimate as a study of the operators on a vertex-transitive graph.

The scope discipline established in earlier rounds holds: §1 and §3.2 both quarantine the CTQW-GNN [12] argument before any result appears, and §6 and §8 do not reclaim it.

The one residual is that the title's new third clause, "and Link Pattern," now carries a meaning the paper supplies only in §5.5's final sentence — "the difference between a graph on which every destination sees the same field and one on which it does not, rather than as the seam of the Walker-delta constellation in particular" — and that reading is the one criterion 3(c) finds unsupported by the paper's own measurement.

Requirements for Rectification.

Fix the title's third clause to whatever survives the mechanism question of criterion 3(c); if the control supports only "some link removal moves this cell," the title should not imply a general factor.

2. Methodological Soundness and Transparency

Assessment: Minor Weaknesses requiring significant rework

Critique. Five of the seven requirements from the previous round are discharged in full and well.

The double-precision recheck (Table 25) covers all three certified cells, reports |S| per arm in each precision, and substitutes the float64 Wilcoxon p back into the correct Holm family of six. All three hold; the seam-cut cell strengthens (raw 0.008 → 0.004, corrected 0.039 → 0.020, nine seeds → ten). Grid 1 now runs on the seam-cut graph (Table 7, 480 runs). The far-node null on the seam-cut graph is given equal weight in §5.5, which applies the paper's own §5.4 argument against itself: "Section 5.4 used exactly this kind of movement to discount the torus majority verdicts, and the same discount applies here." The missing-seed sensitivity is reported exhaustively — seed 1, on which the heat kernel collapsed in three arms and the walk kernel in two — with the corrected p under all four restorations (0.020, 0.029, 0.049, 0.422) and the honest summary "The certification survives three of the four ways of restoring the seed and fails the fourth." §7 now carries the ladder's unequal censoring (0% to 60%) and the direction of its bias. These are model responses.

The defects are confined to the new control.

(a) The random-cut control does not meet the paper's own evidential standard, and the summary sections treat it as if it did. Rule 6 (§4.2) states: "where the observed difference is smaller than [the MDE] the correct statement is that the design cannot certify a difference, not that none exists." Table 19's random-cut row gives d̄ = +0.0300 against an MDE of 0.040. By the paper's own rule the control cannot certify a difference. §5.5 is candid that the p is uncorrected ("the walk kernel leads it at the uncorrected level") but does not mention that the effect falls below the resolution the same table prints beside it, and the abstract and §8 state the conclusion without qualification: "a control that removes as many links at random positions reproduces it, so the reversal follows the loss of vertex-transitivity rather than the seam."

(b) The two arms of the comparison are held to different standards. The seam-cut result is Holm-corrected within a family of six and reported as certified; the random-cut result is a single uncorrected Wilcoxon p of 0.049. "The random cut lands with the seam cut, not with the torus" compares a corrected verdict to an uncorrected one. A 95% interval of [+0.0059, +0.0518] that barely excludes zero would not survive any correction, and none is applied because the cell stands alone.

(c) Rule 5 is satisfied on the seam cut and violated on the random cut, and this is not reported. Table 19's seam-cut block has the walk kernel leading in all four arms (0:7, 1:4, 0:8, 1:5). The random-cut block has the walk kernel leading in three arms and the heat kernel leading in the fourth (softplus, t₀ = 0.5: heat 0.106 ± 0.048, walk 0.116 ± 0.085, vote 3:5, p = 0.844). Rule 5 requires a ranking to hold "in every t arm run at that budget." The paper applies rule 5 nowhere in §5.5, but the asymmetry is exactly the kind of arm-dependence §5.2 is about, and it weakens the claim that the two graphs behave alike.

(d) Grid 1 on the seam-cut graph covers two of four arms. Table 7 runs relu, t₀ = 0.5 and softplus, t₀ = 0.5 only. The cross-graph floor comparison is therefore on half the arm space, and the one cell that clears the floor falls inside it (see criterion 3(d)).

(e) An unexplained dispersion. Table 19's random-cut GCN reports 0.191 ± 0.000 (10) in all four arms, against 0.193 ± 0.004 on the torus, on a graph whose removed links are "drawn per snapshot at random positions." Zero seed dispersion to three decimals on a randomised topology needs a sentence of explanation.

Requirements for Rectification.

Report the random-cut control against rule 6 as the rule is written: state that d̄ = 0.0300 is below its MDE of 0.040 and that the design therefore cannot certify the control's difference, and carry that qualification into the abstract and §8.

Put the two graphs on one footing — either correct the random-cut p within a family, or report both uncorrected alongside the corrected seam-cut value and say which comparison is being made.

Report the per-arm leaders of the random-cut block and note that rule 5 holds on the seam cut and fails on the control.

Run the remaining two t₀ = 2 arms of grid 1 on the seam-cut graph, or state in §5.1 and §8 that the cross-graph floor comparison covers two of four arms.

Explain the random-cut GCN's zero seed dispersion.

3. Data Integrity, Results Presentation, and Analysis

Assessment: Major Deficiencies

Critique. The tables carried over from the previous version reconcile as before: Table 19's per-arm entries for the torus and the seam cut match Tables 10, 12, A.30 and A.31 cell for cell; Tables 13, 21 and 22 reconstruct correctly under Holm with m = 6 at each row's |S|; Table 25's corrected values follow from substituting the float64 raw p into the same family. Three of the previous round's four errors are fixed: 16.4% is now 16.0%, §5.3 carries the fitting-versus-generalisation caveat, and the far-node floor is qualified. The defects below are all in the new material.

Every other column of the two rows agrees exactly (d̄, CI, vote, Wilcoxon p), so these are the same pooled tests with two different resolutions printed. The consequences are not cosmetic. The abstract and §1 and §8 all state that at 1584 satellites the MDE "is 4.3 times the difference of 0.020 the preliminary version reported" — which is 0.087/0.020 = 4.35, and becomes 2.85 under Table 19's 0.057. §5.5's reading of its own recovered result, "its d̄ of +0.0238 sits at its own minimum detectable effect of 0.024," makes that result exactly marginal at 0.024 and comfortably clear (1.4× the resolution) at 0.017. Two of the three sentences a reader will carry away from this paper turn on numbers the paper prints twice with different values.

(b) The proposed mechanism is contradicted by the paper's own measurement of it. §5.5 identifies what the loss of vertex-transitivity provides: "the loss of vertex-transitivity that any such removal of links produces, which is what gives the hop field held-out content." The manuscript measures held-out content as the train–test separation: 0.000 on the torus (§5.3, Table 14: "equal to five decimals in every cell"), 0.009 on the seam-cut graph (§5.3 and §5.5), and 0.001 on the random cut (§5.5). On the variable nominated as the mechanism, the random cut sits nine times closer to the torus than to the seam cut; on the outcome it lands with the seam cut. If held-out content were what moved the cell, the control should have behaved like the torus. The paper reports the 0.001 in the same sentence that asserts "so its instances differ" and draws the opposite conclusion.

(c) §8's first bullet contradicts itself. "Under the preliminary budget the shells of 1584 satellites and more are a floor: the best operator gains at most 2.9% over the constant predictor in any arm at either capacity (Table 5), on the torus and, in 2 arms, on the seam-cut graph, where the largest gain is 5.9% (Table 7)." A floor claim of 2.9% and a reported gain of 5.9% cannot both be asserted of the same statement in one sentence, and 5.9% is also above rule 3's own 5% threshold.

(d) The floor's graph-independence is asserted from a table that shows it weakening. §5.1: "The budget floor is a property of the budget, not of the link pattern." Table 7 against Table 5 in the same two arms, hop field, 1584 satellites: torus 2.8% and 1.0%; seam cut 4.6% and 5.9% — larger by 1.6× and 5.9×. One of twelve large-shell cells on the seam-cut graph clears the 5% floor, against 0 of 24 on the torus, and §5.1 itself says so ("11 of the 12 large-shell cells are below the floor") one sentence before the conclusion that the link pattern does not matter. The gains at 264 satellites tell the same story: 47.0% and 46.3% on the seam cut against 34.6% and 40.3% on the torus.

(e) The precision recheck moves the certified effect by a quarter, and this bears on the marginality argument that §5.5 makes. Table 25: the seam-cut hop/1584 cell has d̄ = +0.0238 in float32 and +0.0293 in float64, a 23% change from rounding alone, with |S| per arm moving from 7/5/8/6 to 9/6/7/8. §5.5's central qualification is that the effect "sits at its own minimum detectable effect of 0.024." An effect size that a change of arithmetic moves by 23% cannot be located against a threshold to two decimal places, and §5.5 and §5.7 are not connected.

Requirements for Rectification.

Regenerate Table 19's MDE column from the same routine that produces Tables 13 and 21, resolve which values are correct, and state the resulting ratio in the abstract, §1, §5.3, §5.5 and §8 consistently.

Withdraw or re-derive the held-out-content mechanism. Either report that the random cut's separation (0.001) does not match the seam cut's (0.009) and that the mechanism is therefore not identified, or run a control that varies held-out content while holding link count fixed.

Rewrite §8's first bullet so the floor claim and the 5.9% gain are not asserted together.

Replace §5.1's "not of the link pattern" with what Table 7 shows: the floor holds on both graphs at 5%, but the gains at 1584 satellites are up to six times larger on the seam-cut graph, and one cell clears the threshold there.

State in §5.5 that the certified effect moves from +0.0238 to +0.0293 between precisions, and reconcile that with the "sits at its own MDE" reading.

4. Discussion, Interpretation, and Conclusions

Assessment: Acceptable

Critique. §8 is now written as a list of statements each keyed to the table it is read from, prefaced by "nothing is claimed that is not in one of them" — the correct answer to the recurring problem of earlier rounds, and it works: five of the six bullets are exact. §5.5's own reading remains appropriately restrained ("The reading is not that the walk kernel is the better operator on seam-cut graphs"), and it now volunteers the counter-evidence: the far-node metric certifies 0 of six on that graph, the cell rests on nine seeds, the learned t "does not explain the reversal … the kernel with the longer learned time loses the cell." §7 lists the per-cell test's lack of power, the ladder's censoring with its direction, the post-hoc floor, and the misread flag with its detection method.

What §7 does not list is anything about the new control: not that its effect is below its MDE, not that its p is uncorrected, not that one arm reverses, not that its train–test separation does not match the seam cut's. In a threats section that now itemises the weaknesses of every other component, the newest and most interpretively consequential piece of evidence is the one with no entry.

Requirements for Rectification.

Add the random-cut control to §7 with the four qualifications above.

6. Writing Quality, Precision, and Conciseness

Assessment: Minor Weaknesses requiring significant rework

Critique. The manuscript has grown to 40 pages across three revisions and remains readable; the tables are well captioned and Table 19's three-graph layout is the right way to present the control. §8's table-keyed structure should be adopted by others.

The precision problem has, for the third consecutive round, migrated into the newest material. In the previous version the errors were in the passages responding to the round before; here they are in Table 19, §5.5's mechanism sentence and §8's first bullet, all written for this revision. Each round's carried-over text is now clean; each round's new text arrives with a claim ahead of its table. That pattern is worth the author's attention as a process matter, because it is the single thing standing between this manuscript and acceptance.

Requirements for Rectification.

Apply to the new material the check §8 already implies: for every quantitative sentence written for this revision, name the table it is read from and verify the value against that table before submission.

Incisive Critical Questions for the Authors

Table 19 gives the pooled MDE of the 1584-satellite hop cell as 0.057 on the torus and 0.017 on the seam-cut graph; Tables 13 and 21 give 0.087 and 0.024 for the same pooled tests, with every other column identical. Which is correct, and does the abstract's "4.3 times" become 2.85, or does §5.5's "sits at its own minimum detectable effect of 0.024" become "exceeds it by 40%"?

Rule 6 states that where an observed difference is smaller than the MDE, "the correct statement is that the design cannot certify a difference." The random-cut control gives d̄ = +0.0300 against an MDE of 0.040. On what basis does the abstract report that it "reproduces" the seam-cut reversal, and what does the control establish under the paper's own rule?

§5.5 attributes the reversal to the loss of vertex-transitivity "which is what gives the hop field held-out content," and reports that separation as 0.000 on the torus, 0.001 on the random cut and 0.009 on the seam-cut graph. If held-out content is the mechanism, why does the graph with 0.001 behave like the one with 0.009 rather than the one with 0.000?

On the seam-cut graph all four arms have the walk kernel leading; on the random cut, three do and the softplus t₀ = 0.5 arm has the heat kernel leading at 3:5, p = 0.844. Given that rule 5 exists precisely to catch arm-dependence, why is it applied nowhere in §5.5, and does the control satisfy it?

§8's first bullet asserts a floor of "at most 2.9%" and reports a seam-cut gain of 5.9% in the same sentence, and Table 7's hop/1584 gains exceed the torus's by factors of 1.6 and 5.9 in the same two arms. On what evidence does §5.1 conclude that the budget floor is "not [a property] of the link pattern"?

Concluding Remarks

Recommendation: Major Revisions.

The response to the previous round is close to exemplary. Of seven requirements, six were discharged in full: the torus's unreality governs the title, abstract and introduction rather than a footnote; the 4.3× resolution ratio is stated where the null is claimed; the two certified torus cells are labelled as dependent tests at the design's floor, one on a task with no held-out content; the far-node null on the seam-cut graph is given the same weight as the result it undercuts, with the paper applying its own §5.4 discount argument against its own finding; the missing seed is identified and the corrected p reported under all four ways of restoring it, with the honest summary that three of four preserve the certification; and all three certified cells were rerun in double precision, where they hold. Grid 1 was extended to the seam-cut graph, the ladder's censoring direction was added to §7, and 16.4% was corrected to 16.0%. The seventh requirement — separate topology from task nature — was met by running a control that overturned the author's own interpretation, and the author changed the title rather than absorb the result quietly. Few revisions in this reviewer's experience run a control that costs them their explanation and then lead with the outcome.

What holds it short is that the new material repeats, in miniature, the pattern the rest of the manuscript has now eliminated. Table 19 prints two minimum detectable effects that contradict Tables 13 and 21 for the same cells, and both of the paper's most-quoted figures — the abstract's 4.3× and §5.5's "sits at its own MDE" — turn on which value is right. The control itself is below the resolution the same table prints beside it, is compared uncorrected against a corrected result, reverses in one of four arms, and separates train from test by 0.001 where the graph it is said to match separates by 0.009 — so the mechanism the abstract and the new title assert is contradicted by the paper's own measurement of that mechanism. And §8's first bullet states a 2.9% floor and a 5.9% gain in one sentence.

The work required is one table regenerated, one mechanism claim re-derived or withdrawn, four qualifications added to §7 that are already computable from Table 19, and two sentences corrected. No new runs are needed except the two remaining grid-1 arms on the seam-cut graph, which are cheap. With those done, this would be a reproducibility audit of unusual quality — one that found a false result of its own, then a misread flag that invalidated the setting of 2920 runs, then a control that took away its explanation of the one result it recovered, and reported each in the abstract. The standard the manuscript now applies to its inherited text needs only to be applied to the text it writes next.
